A.  The S-timer level in `Machine::fresh_mip`  (lib.rs:5601)
============================================================
The rule (Boundary: "the levels this boundary samples … the SBI timer"; `sync_sbi_timer`,
lib.rs:5336, is the reference and is independent code): with the built-in SBI and a CLINT,

      STIP (mip bit 5, value 32)  <=>  mtime >= stimecmp        — a LEVEL, set AND cleared;
      without the built-in SBI the bit is left as it was.

A `>=` is decided by three points. What a test must contain to kill each mutant, with the
clock at T:

  A1  `>=` -> `>`            differs only at stimecmp == T.   Needs a case stimecmp = T, SBI on,
                              and an assertion that bit 5 is ON there.
  A2  `>=` -> `==`           differs at every stimecmp < T.   Needs a case stimecmp < T (one tick
                              past, T-1, is the sharp one; 0 is the far one), SBI on, bit 5 ON.
  A3  `mtime + 1 >=`         differs only at stimecmp == T+1. Needs EXACTLY T+1 (not "far
                              ahead"), SBI on, and an assertion that bit 5 is OFF there.

  So the minimum is {T-1, T, T+1} under SBI-on, each with an assertion that would see bit 5.
  "Far ahead" (u64::MAX, the cancelled sentinel) and 0 add the two ends but replace none of
  the three.

  A4  What the assertion must compare against. Two things, and both matter:
      (a) `fresh_mip()` taken BEFORE the sync == `mip` AFTER the machine's own level sync
          (the acceptance sentence). This alone is blind to the same error made in
          `sync_sbi_timer` (round 4's probe P3b).
      (b) the rule itself, with the expected bit computed from the test's own literals
          (`stimecmp <= T`), not from `Clint::mtip()`, `fresh_mip()` or the synced `mip`.
      If only (a) exists the test is "equal to the sync", not "right". If (b) is computed
      from a machine accessor it is self-licking. I expect both, per the worker's entry; I
      will check where the right-hand side of (b) comes from.

  A5  Independence of the two timers. If `mtimecmp` and `stimecmp` are always on the same side
      of T, a `fresh_mip` that derives STIP from the machine timer (`c.mtip()` / `mtimecmp`)
      passes. The two positions must vary independently (5 x 5 per the worker's entry).

  A6  Level, not latch. Some case must enter with STIP stale-ON and require OFF, and some
      with stale-OFF and require ON, both under SBI-on; and under SBI-off the stale bit must
      be required to SURVIVE in both polarities (otherwise "no `builtin_sbi` guard" and
      "set-only / clear-only" mutants pass).

B.  The cap on owed waiting time after an idle jump  (time.rs:229-232; mutant M1)
=================================================================================
What the Boundary says, in my words. Two quantities bound what an idle jump may give a guest:
the host clock (an idle jump never passes it) and, after work, `owed + max_busy_lag`.
"Owed" is waiting time the host clock has seen and the guest clock has not been given because
a deadline stood in the way; it "stays owed and survives later work". It follows that owed
time can never exceed what the host clock is ahead of the guest by — there is nothing else for
it to be made of — and the moment to judge that is AFTER this consultation's jump, because
the jump is the handing over. With L = (host ceiling − guest time before the jump) and
j = the jump:

      owed' = min(owed − j, L − j)          (code: `.saturating_sub(limited − current)`
                                                    `.min(ceiling.saturating_sub(limited))`)
      M1:     min(owed − j, L)              — the lead as it was BEFORE the jump.

They differ iff owed > L and j > 0: the guest's own (retire-derived) clock gained on the host
clock since the time became owed, and then an idle jump happened. Then M1 leaves min(owed, L)
− … too much, up to j, on the books: a debt that has just been paid, which the next busy
stretch skips ON TOP of its bound — against "at most `max_busy_lag` of the time it fell
behind may be skipped".

The scenario of the worker's entry, with my numbers derived from the Boundary before seeing
the test (time in ms; at the machine's 10 MHz timebase 1 ms = 10,000 ticks; guest time G,
host ceiling H, both starting at 0; bound 50 ms):

  step 1  wfi, deadline G+2. Host has not moved: jump 0, guest waits.           owed 0
  step 2  host oversleeps: H = 3002. Consult, not worked: owed += 3002; ceiling 3002;
          deadline 2 -> jump 2 (G = 2); owed = 3002 − 2 = 3000; lead after = 3000.
                                                                    owed 3000   (== lead)
  step 3  the handler runs 2200 ms of guest time in 200 ms of host time:
          G = 2202, H = 3202, lead L = 1000.  (owed 3000 > L: the M1 condition.)
  step 4a consult, worked, NOTHING in the way (deadline = u64::MAX or beyond H):
          bounded = 2202 + 3000 + 50 = 5252 > ceiling 3202, so no clamp, the anchor does
          not move; jump = 1000 (G = 3202 = H).
              right:  owed = min(3000 − 1000, 3202 − 3202) = 0
              M1:     owed = min(2000, 3202 − 2202)        = 1000
  step 4b the same state, a deadline 300 ms ahead (2502): jump = 300 (G = 2502).
              right:  owed = min(2700, 3202 − 2502) = 700
              M1:     owed = min(2700, 1000)        = 1000
  step 5  a busy stretch during which the host runs far ahead (say +10 s) and no deadline is
          in the way; consult, worked:  jump = owed + 50 exactly.
              after 4a:  right   50 ms      M1  1050 ms
              after 4b:  right  750 ms      M1  1050 ms

  What the test must assert to pin the RULE and not just this mutant:
  B1  the returned guest time at step 4 (the jump itself: 1000 / 300) — M1 does not change

  B2  `owed_wait_ticks()` after step 4: exactly 0 (4a) and exactly 700 ms (4b), as literals

  B3  the BEHAVIOUR: the step-5 jump is exactly owed + 50 ms (the getter is read by tests

  B4  both 4a and 4b. They are not redundant: 4a alone cannot tell "pay, then cap" from

  B5  the precondition actually holds in the test: owed (3000) > lead (1000) with j > 0. If
